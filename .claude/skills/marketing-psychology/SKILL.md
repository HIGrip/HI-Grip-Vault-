## Dual Process Theory — Systeem 1 & Systeem 2

Kahneman's foundational framework: the brain operates in two modes simultaneously.

**Systeem 1 — Fast, Automatic, Emotional**
Operates unconsciously, effortlessly, and continuously in the background. Drives snap judgments, gut feelings, and habitual behavior. Responds to visuals, emotion, contrast, and familiar patterns.
Marketing application: The majority of consumer decisions are Systeem 1. Design packaging, ads, and landing pages to be instantly felt, not thought. Emotional resonance beats rational argument. A striking visual, a familiar jingle, or a relatable face triggers Systeem 1 before the customer is aware of it.

**Systeem 2 — Slow, Deliberate, Analytical**
Conscious, effortful, and limited. Activates when decisions feel important, unfamiliar, or complex. Relies on information, comparisons, and reasoning.
Marketing application: Detailed product specs, comparison tables, and in-depth reviews serve Systeem 2 buyers. Use these for high-consideration purchases. But always lead with Systeem 1 — you need to earn the slow thinking by winning the fast feeling first.

The critical insight: most buyers believe they're using Systeem 2 while actually being steered by Systeem 1. Design for both — create emotional resonance, then justify with logic.

## Foundational Thinking Models

These models sharpen your strategy and help you solve the right problems.

**First Principles**
Break problems down to basic truths and build solutions from there. Instead of copying competitors, ask "why" repeatedly to find root causes. Use the 5 Whys technique to tunnel down to what really matters.
Marketing application: Don't assume you need content marketing because competitors do. Ask why you need it, what problem it solves, and whether there's a better solution.

**Jobs to Be Done**
People don't buy products—they "hire" them to get a job done. Focus on the outcome customers want, not features.
Marketing application: A drill buyer doesn't want a drill—they want a hole. Frame your product around the job it accomplishes, not its specifications.

**Circle of Competence**
Know what you're good at and stay within it. Venture outside only with proper learning or expert help.
Marketing application: Don't chase every channel. Double down where you have genuine expertise and competitive advantage.

**Inversion**
Instead of asking "How do I succeed?", ask "What would guarantee failure?" Then avoid those things.
Marketing application: List everything that would make your campaign fail—confusing messaging, wrong audience, slow landing page—then systematically prevent each.

**Occam's Razor**
The simplest explanation is usually correct. Avoid overcomplicating strategies.
Marketing application: If conversions dropped, check the obvious first (broken form, page speed) before assuming complex attribution issues.

**Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule)**
Roughly 80% of results come from 20% of efforts. Identify and focus on the vital few.
Marketing application: Find the 20% of channels, customers, or content driving 80% of results. Cut or reduce the rest.

**Local vs. Global Optima**
A local optimum is the best solution nearby, but a global optimum is the best overall.
Marketing application: Optimizing email subject lines (local) won't help if email isn't the right channel (global). Zoom out before zooming in.

**Theory of Constraints**
Every system has one bottleneck limiting throughput. Find and fix that constraint before optimizing elsewhere.
Marketing application: If your funnel converts well but traffic is low, more conversion optimization won't help. Fix the traffic bottleneck first.

**Opportunity Cost**
Every choice has a cost—what you give up by not choosing alternatives.
Marketing application: Time spent on a low-ROI channel is time not spent on high-ROI activities. Always compare against alternatives.

**Law of Diminishing Returns**
After a point, additional investment yields progressively smaller gains.
Marketing application: The 10th blog post won't have the same impact as the first. Know when to diversify rather than double down.

**Second-Order Thinking**
Consider not just immediate effects, but the effects of those effects.
Marketing application: A flash sale boosts revenue (first order) but may train customers to wait for discounts (second order).

**Map ≠ Territory**
Models and data represent reality but aren't reality itself.
Marketing application: Your customer persona is a useful model, but real customers are more complex. Stay in touch with actual users.

**Probabilistic Thinking**
Think in probabilities, not certainties. Estimate likelihoods and plan for multiple outcomes.
Marketing application: Don't bet everything on one campaign. Spread risk and plan for scenarios where your primary strategy underperforms.

**Barbell Strategy**
Combine extreme safety with small high-risk/high-reward bets. Avoid the mediocre middle.
Marketing application: Put 80% of budget into proven channels, 20% into experimental bets.

## Understanding Buyers & Human Psychology

These models explain how customers think, decide, and behave.

**Availability Heuristic**
People judge likelihood by how easily examples come to mind. This is personal: someone who just saw a car accident overestimates crash risk; someone who just saw a success story overestimates that product's odds.
Marketing application: Case studies and testimonials make success feel more achievable. Make positive outcomes vivid and easy to imagine. Frequent brand exposure keeps you top of mind when the purchase moment arrives.

**Representativeness Heuristic**
People judge probability by how much something resembles a category stereotype, not by base rates.
Marketing application: Design products, spokespeople, and brand visuals to match the category expectations your audience holds — then differentiate within that frame. A supplement that looks clinical signals credibility before a word is read.

**Gambler's Fallacy**
The tendency to see patterns in random sequences. After three identical outcomes people expect the opposite — but each event is independent.
Marketing application: Don't let a losing campaign streak trigger panic pivots or a winning streak trigger complacency. Past performance does not predict future A/B test outcomes. Each experiment is independent.

**Anchoring Effect**
The first number or piece of information someone sees heavily influences all subsequent judgments.
Marketing application: Always display the higher reference price before revealing your offer. Show competitor pricing, original price, or premium tier first. The anchor sets the frame; your actual price then feels like a deal.

**Confirmation Bias**
People seek information that confirms existing beliefs.
Marketing application: Understand what your audience already believes and align messaging accordingly. Fighting existing beliefs head-on rarely works — mirror their worldview, then shift it incrementally.

**Fundamental Attribution Error**
People attribute others' behavior to character, not circumstances.
Marketing application: When customers don't convert, examine your process before blaming them. The problem is usually situational, not personal.

**Mere Exposure Effect**
People prefer things they've seen before. Familiarity breeds liking.
Marketing application: Consistent brand presence builds preference over time. Repetition across channels creates comfort and trust.

**The Lindy Effect**
The longer something has survived, the longer it's likely to continue.
Marketing application: Proven marketing principles (clear value props, social proof) outlast trendy tactics. Don't abandon fundamentals for fads.

**Mimetic Desire**
People want things because others want them. Desire is socially contagious.
Marketing application: Show that desirable people want your product. Waitlists, exclusivity, and social proof trigger mimetic desire.

**Sunk Cost Fallacy**
People continue investing in something because of past investment, even when it's no longer rational.
Marketing application: Know when to kill underperforming campaigns. Past spend shouldn't justify future spend if results aren't there.

**Endowment Effect**
People value things more once they own them.
Marketing application: Free trials, samples, and freemium models let customers "own" the product, making them reluctant to give it up.

**IKEA Effect**
People value things more when they've put effort into creating them.
Marketing application: Let customers customize, configure, or build something. Their investment increases perceived value and commitment.

**Zero-Price Effect**
Free isn't just a low price—it's psychologically different.
Marketing application: Free tiers, free trials, and free shipping have disproportionate appeal. The jump from €1 to €0 is bigger than €2 to €1.

**Hyperbolic Discounting / Present Bias**
People strongly prefer immediate rewards over future ones.
Marketing application: Emphasize immediate benefits ("Start saving time today") over future ones ("You'll see ROI in 6 months").

**Status-Quo Bias**
People prefer the current state of affairs. Change requires effort and feels risky.
Marketing application: Reduce friction to switch. Make the transition feel safe and easy. "Import your data in one click."

**Default Effect**
People tend to accept pre-selected options. Defaults are powerful.
Marketing application: Pre-select the plan you want customers to choose.

**Paradox of Choice**
Too many options overwhelm and paralyze.
Marketing application: Limit options. Three pricing tiers beat seven. Recommend a single "best for most" option.

**Goal-Gradient Effect**
People accelerate effort as they approach a goal.
Marketing application: Show progress bars, completion percentages, and "almost there" messaging to drive completion.

**Peak-End Rule**
People judge experiences by the peak and the end, not the average.
Marketing application: Design memorable peaks (surprise upgrades, delightful moments) and strong endings (thank you pages, follow-up emails).

**Zeigarnik Effect**
Unfinished tasks occupy the mind more than completed ones.
Marketing application: "You're 80% done" creates pull to finish. Incomplete profiles, abandoned carts, and cliffhangers leverage this.

**Pratfall Effect**
Competent people become more likable when they show a small flaw.
Marketing application: Admitting a weakness ("We're not the cheapest, but...") can increase trust and differentiation.

**Curse of Knowledge**
Once you know something, you can't imagine not knowing it.
Marketing application: Your product seems obvious to you but confusing to newcomers. Test copy with people unfamiliar with your space.

**Mental Accounting**
People treat money differently based on its source or intended use.
Marketing application: Frame costs in favorable mental accounts. "€3/day" feels different than "€90/month."

**Regret Aversion**
People avoid actions that might cause regret.
Marketing application: Address regret directly. Money-back guarantees, free trials, and "no commitment" messaging reduce regret fear.

**Bandwagon Effect / Social Proof**
People follow what others are doing.
Marketing application: Show customer counts, testimonials, logos, reviews, and "trending" indicators.

## Social Influence & Group Dynamics

**Conformisme (Asch Effect)**
When surrounded by a consensus view, people often abandon their own correct judgment to fit in. Two mechanisms drive this: normatieve invloed (fear of social rejection) and informatieve invloed (belief that the group knows better). The effect is strongest in ambiguous situations and disappears when even one person breaks from the consensus.
Marketing application: Reviews, bestseller labels, and crowd-sourced ratings reduce the risk of going against the grain. "8 out of 10 customers chose this option" makes the safe choice feel obvious. One strong dissenting voice in reviews can paradoxically increase overall trust — it breaks the group unanimity and makes remaining positive reviews feel more authentic.

**Social Modeling & Competentie-Cues (Stereotype Content Model)**
Consumers don't imitate everyone — they imitate people they perceive as competent. The Stereotype Content Model shows we evaluate others on two axes: warmth and competence. Competence is inferred from visible status cues: payment method, clothing, professional context.
Marketing application: Show aspirational customers using your product — people whose competence level your target audience admires. A premium context, professional setting, or expert credentials signal that this is what smart people choose. For indulgent or pleasurable products, sensory cues outperform social cues; social modeling works best for considered, healthy, or status-relevant choices.

**Deindividuatie & Anonimiteit**
People behave differently when anonymous or absorbed into a group identity. Individuality fades, group norms dominate, and the threshold for extreme behavior drops.
Marketing application: Community-driven brands benefit from shared identity ("we're all athletes here") amplifying in-group behavior. In user-generated content and reviews, anonymous feedback is more extreme — don't over-weight outliers. Personalization (named accounts, personal progress tracking) counters deindividuatie and keeps members accountable to their own commitments.

## Motivation & Drive

**Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Motivation**
Intrinsic motivation comes from internal drivers: enjoyment, curiosity, mastery, personal values. It is durable and self-sustaining. Extrinsic motivation comes from external rewards: discounts, badges, rankings. It works short-term but erodes when removed.
Critical — Overjustification Effect: Adding external rewards to an already intrinsically motivated behavior can destroy that intrinsic motivation. A customer who loves your product for its own sake may stop caring if it becomes transactional.
Marketing application: Use discounts sparingly for your most loyal customers — you risk turning passion into a price calculation. Loyalty programs that reward identity and belonging (intrinsic) outlast pure points-for-purchases systems (extrinsic).

**Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)**
Sustained motivation requires three psychological needs:
- Autonomie: the feeling of having real choice and control
- Verbondenheid: belonging, connection with others who share the experience
- Competentie: feeling capable and increasingly effective

Marketing application: Give customers genuine choices, not fake ones. Build communities and shared identity around your brand. Design onboarding so customers feel progressively more capable. Products that fulfill all three needs create deep loyalty that resists competitor offers.

**Expectancy Theory (Vroom)**
Motivation = Verwachting × Instrumentaliteit × Valentie
- Verwachting: "If I try, can I succeed?" (perceived self-efficacy)
- Instrumentaliteit: "If I succeed, will I get the reward?" (trust in the promise)
- Valentie: "Is the reward valuable to me?" (personal relevance)

Marketing application: All three must be high for action. A compelling offer fails if the customer doubts they can use the product (low Verwachting), doesn't trust the brand to deliver (low Instrumentaliteit), or doesn't care about the benefit (low Valentie). Diagnose which factor is blocking conversion before optimizing copy.

**Dopamine & Variabele Beloning**
Dopamine is the anticipation molecule, not the pleasure molecule. It spikes on the expectation of a reward, not the receipt of it. Uncertainty maximizes dopamine release — this is why variable reward schedules are so compelling: the unpredictability keeps the dopamine system engaged.
Marketing application: Build variability into engagement loops. Surprise discounts outperform scheduled ones. Mystery bundles, randomized gifts, and unpredictable content drops create anticipation-driven engagement. Never make rewards completely predictable — the dopamine spike disappears once the pattern is learned.

**Approach & Avoidance Drives**
Humans automatically move toward positive, attractive stimuli (approach) and away from threatening or negative stimuli (avoidance). These are pre-conscious Systeem 1 responses.
Marketing application: Design all touchpoints to trigger approach behavior. Warm colors, open body language, smiling faces, and expansive imagery signal safety and attractiveness. Fear-based marketing works only when paired with a clear, easy path to relief — without the solution, fear creates paralysis, not action.

## Influencing Behavior & Persuasion (Cialdini's 7 Principles)

Robert Cialdini's principles are scientifically validated influence mechanisms. Use them ethically — manufactured scarcity or fake authority destroys trust and causes lasting brand damage.

**Wederkerigheid (Reciprocity)**
People feel obligated to return what they've received. Unexpected and personal giving works strongest. A restaurant that gave one mint with the bill increased tips 3%; two mints increased tips 14%; two mints plus a compliment increased tips 23%.
Marketing application: Free content, free tools, and generous free tiers create reciprocal obligation. Give genuine value before asking for anything. The gift must feel authentic — a cynical freebie backfires.

**Sympathie (Liking)**
People buy from people they like. Four drivers: physical attractiveness, similarity, compliments, and familiarity through contact and cooperation.
Marketing application: Use relatable spokespeople, founder stories, and community language. Speak the exact language of your target group. Influencer collaborations work when the influencer genuinely uses the product — authenticity is the precondition for liking.

**Sociale Bewijskracht (Social Proof)**
Recommendations from others are more convincing than your own claims. The effect is strongest when the proof-giver is similar to the buyer.
Marketing application: Show reviews, testimonials, star ratings, and volume of customers. Trustpilot scores, media mentions, and "as tested best by" labels reduce purchase risk. Match the proof to the buyer: expert proof for expertise-driven decisions, peer proof for lifestyle and taste.

**Autoriteit (Authority)**
People defer to experts and authority figures — often automatically, without verifying credentials.
Marketing application: Feature expert endorsements, certifications, quality marks, awards, and "featured in" logos. Titles, credentials, and professional context signal authority before a word is read.

**Schaarste (Scarcity)**
People want what is scarce or temporarily available. FOMO (fear of missing out) drives urgency. Real scarcity is far more powerful than manufactured scarcity.
Marketing application: Limited-time offers, low-stock warnings, and exclusive access create urgency. Countdown timers, "only 3 left," and limited-edition drops work — but only when genuine. Fake scarcity destroys the trust you need for everything else.

**Commitment & Consistentie**
Once people have committed to something, they want to remain consistent with that commitment. Small commitments predict large ones.
Marketing application: Newsletter signup → purchase → review → upsell. Each small yes increases the probability of the next yes. Multi-step forms, preference quizzes, and save-your-progress features build commitment before the sale.

**Eenheid (Unity — 7th Principle)**
Shared identity, not just similarity, drives the deepest influence. We-feeling based on community, shared values, or group membership. Being part of the same "us" is more powerful than merely being similar.
Marketing application: Membership programs, brand communities, and insider content create Unity. Build belonging, not just affinity. "This is for people like us" goes deeper than "this is for people like you."

## Pricing Psychology

**Charm Pricing / Left-Digit Effect**
Prices ending in 9 seem significantly lower. €99 feels much cheaper than €100.
Marketing application: Use .99 or .95 endings for value-focused products.

**Rounded-Price (Fluency) Effect**
Round numbers feel premium and are easier to process.
Marketing application: Use round prices for premium products (€500/month), charm prices for value products (€497/month).

**Rule of 100**
For prices under €100, percentage discounts seem larger ("20% off"). For prices over €100, absolute discounts seem larger ("€50 off").

**Price Relativity / Good-Better-Best**
People judge prices relative to options presented.
Marketing application: Three tiers where the middle is your target. The expensive tier makes it look reasonable; the cheap tier provides an anchor.

**Weber's Law — Just Noticeable Difference (JND)**
The minimum perceptible difference is a constant ratio of the original stimulus, not an absolute amount. A €5 discount on a €10 product is immediately felt; the same €5 on a €500 product is invisible.
Marketing application: Price reductions must exceed the JND threshold to be perceived as meaningful. Design decisions — logo tweaks, color changes, packaging updates — that fall below the JND will go unnoticed. Decide consciously: do you want the change noticed, or not?

**Hobsons Choice Plus 1**
Hobson's Choice is a single option or nothing — no real choice. Adding one unattractive alternative creates the illusion of free choice while virtually guaranteeing the desired outcome is selected.
Marketing application: Pair your main offer with an obviously inferior option. The contrast removes resistance and increases conversion because the customer feels they're choosing, not being forced. "Full access" vs. "limited access" makes full access feel like the smart pick.

**Decoy Effect**
A third, inferior option makes one of the two real options look more attractive.
Marketing application: Small coffee €2, medium €4, large €4.20 — the medium is the decoy that makes the large look like an obvious upgrade. Use strategically in pricing tiers.

**Loss Aversion / Prospect Theory**
Losses feel roughly twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good. People are risk-averse for gains, risk-seeking for losses.
Marketing application: Frame offers in terms of what is lost by not acting. "Don't miss out" beats "You could gain." Free trial periods play on loss aversion: stopping feels like losing something you already had.

## Perception & Attention

**Priming & Subliminale Messaging**
A stimulus below or at the edge of conscious awareness influences response to a later stimulus — without the person connecting the two. This is the mechanism behind environmental design and ambient marketing.
Marketing application: Music tempo, scent, color temperature, and imagery prime emotional state before purchase. A slow-tempo soundtrack increases browsing time and spend. A scent associated with the product category increases purchase likelihood. Loading animations, background imagery, and color palette all prime before a word is read.

**Feature Integration Theory — Pop-Out vs. Conjunction Search (Treisman)**
Simple search (pop-out): a target with one unique feature (a red dot among blue dots) is found instantly, regardless of how many distractors exist. Processed automatically by Systeem 1.
Conjunction search: a target that combines multiple features requires conscious, serial scanning. Search time rises linearly with distractors. Systeem 2 required.
Marketing application: The most important element in any visual — CTA button, key message, product — must pop out via a single distinctive feature. Use one dominant differentiating element: color, size, or shape. If the eye has to search for it, you've already lost the customer.

**Gestalt Psychology**
The brain actively constructs meaning and fills in missing information. Four principles:
- Nabijheid (Proximity): elements close together are perceived as a group
- Gelijksoortigheid (Similarity): similar elements are grouped together
- Geslotenheid (Closure): incomplete shapes are perceived as complete — the brain fills gaps
- Figuur-Achtergrond (Figure-Ground): the brain separates foreground from background

Marketing application: Use Proximity to group related product information. Use Similarity for visual consistency across a product line. Use Closure to create memorable logos the brain "completes." Audit for accidental Figure-Ground ambiguity that confuses the message.

**Stroop Effect — Cognitive Congruence**
When a word and its color conflict (RED written in blue), processing slows — the automatic reading process interferes with conscious color perception.
Marketing application: Incongruent communication increases cognitive load and erodes trust. Congruent communication — where all cues align — processes faster and feels more credible. Audit all touchpoints: does the visual match the message? Does the tone match the price point? Does the product image match the copy?

**A/B Testing & Perceptual Differences**
Google tested 41 shades of blue for search result links, serving each to 1% of users. A slightly more purple-tinted blue generated an estimated €200M in additional annual revenue from click behavior alone.
Marketing application: Perceptual differences that feel negligible at the individual level can produce significant aggregate effects at scale. A/B test every element where perception matters: color, button shape, headline length, image choice. Never assume a difference is too small to test — and never assume a winning variant will transfer between devices, screens, or audiences.

## Memory & Learning

**Memory Architecture**
Working memory: limited capacity, active processing, temporary — information decays in seconds without rehearsal.
Long-term memory has two branches:
- Declaratief (explicit): Semantisch (facts, concepts, brand associations) and Episodisch (personal memories of specific events and experiences)
- Non-declaratief (implicit): skills, habits, conditioned responses — operates without awareness

Marketing application: Brand associations live in semantisch geheugen. Brand experiences live in episodisch geheugen. Episodic memories carry emotional weight — they're the campaigns people remember years later. Design for both: clear associative messaging (semantic) and memorable brand moments (episodic).

**Klassieke Conditionering (Pavlov)**
A neutral stimulus becomes a conditioned stimulus by repeated pairing with a stimulus that already produces a response. After conditioning, the neutral stimulus alone produces the response.
Marketing application: Brand sounds, colors, and scents become conditioned stimuli through repeated pairing with positive experiences. The McDonald's jingle, the Intel chime, a signature store scent — these trigger emotional associations before any product information is processed. Consistent sensory cues across all touchpoints deepen the conditioned response over time.

**Operante Conditionering (Skinner)**
Behavior is shaped by its consequences. Reinforcement increases likelihood of recurrence; punishment decreases it.
- Positieve versterking: add a reward after the behavior (warm thank-you confirmation page)
- Negatieve versterking: remove an aversive stimulus after the behavior (removing ads for premium subscribers)
- Positieve bestraffing: add an aversive consequence (late payment fees)
- Negatieve bestraffing: remove a desired reward (losing loyalty points after inactivity)

Marketing application: Design every post-purchase touchpoint to positively reinforce the purchase decision. Gamification, loyalty programs, and subscription models all run on operant conditioning. Punishment-based mechanics suppress behavior but generate resentment — use them sparingly and fairly.

**Beloningsschemas — Reinforcement Schedules**

| Schema | Beschrijving | Voorbeeld | Respons | Uitdoving |
|--------|-------------|-----------|---------|-----------|
| Continu | Elke keer beloning | Automaat geeft altijd koffie | Langzaam | Snel |
| Vast Ratio (FR) | Beloning na vast aantal acties | Koffiekaart: 10e gratis | Snel | Gemiddeld |
| Variabel Ratio (VR) | Beloning na variabel aantal acties | Gokautomaat, social media likes | Zeer snel | Langzaam |
| Vast Interval (FI) | Beloning na vaste tijdsperiode | Maandsalaris | Gemiddeld | Gemiddeld |
| Variabel Interval (VI) | Beloning na variabele tijdsperiode | E-mail checken | Snel | Langzaam |

Variable Ratio is the most powerful and most resistant to extinction. The unpredictability keeps engagement high because the dopamine anticipation system never habituates.
Marketing application: Push notifications with variable timing outperform scheduled ones. Surprise gifts outperform predictable loyalty rewards. Content drops at unpredictable intervals keep audiences checking back. Design for variability where sustained engagement is the goal.

**Context-Afhankelijk Geheugen**
Information learned in a specific context (physical environment, emotional state, sensory cues) is retrieved most easily in that same context.
Marketing application: Consistent use of brand scents, music, color, and language across all channels means the customer is more likely to recall your brand at the moment of purchase. Omnichannel consistency is a memory strategy, not just a brand strategy.

**Semantisch Netwerk & Merkassociaties (Rapaille)**
Knowledge is stored as a network of concepts connected by associations. Activating one concept spreads activation through the network. Brands occupy nodes in this network — surrounded by emotional, sensory, and cultural associations that consumers cannot always consciously articulate.

The Jeep Wrangler Case: Chrysler considered making the Jeep more luxurious based on focus group feedback. Rapaille analyzed the deep associations consumers actually held — stories of freedom, wilderness, the American frontier. He identified the cultural code: HORSE. Not luxury. His advice — restore the round headlights, remove luxury features, bring back the removable doors — directly contradicted what consumers said they wanted, but reflected what the brand meant in their semantic network. Sales recovered immediately.

Marketing application: Don't ask customers what they want — ask what the brand means to them. Focus groups and surveys surface Systeem 2 rationalizations, not Systeem 1 associations. The real brand lives in the semantic network. Never pull the brand in a direction that conflicts with its deep associative code — the consumer's Systeem 1 will reject it even if their Systeem 2 agrees with your rationale.

## Design & Delivery Models

**Hick's Law**
Decision time increases with the number and complexity of choices.
Marketing application: Simplify choices. One clear CTA beats three. Fewer form fields beat more.

**AIDA Funnel**
Attention → Interest → Desire → Action.
Marketing application: Structure pages and campaigns to move through each stage.

**Rule of 7**
Prospects need roughly 7 touchpoints before converting.
Marketing application: Build multi-touch campaigns across channels. Retargeting, email sequences, and consistent presence compound.

**Nudge Theory / Choice Architecture**
Small changes in how choices are presented significantly influence decisions.
Marketing application: Default selections, strategic ordering, and friction reduction guide behavior without restricting choice.

**BJ Fogg Behavior Model**
Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt. All three must be present for action.
Marketing application: High motivation but hard to do = won't happen. Easy to do but no prompt = won't happen. Design for all three.

**EAST Framework**
Make desired behaviors: Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely.

**COM-B Model**
Behavior requires: Capability, Opportunity, Motivation.

**Activation Energy**
The initial energy required to start something. High activation energy prevents action.
Marketing application: Reduce starting friction. Pre-fill forms, offer templates, show quick wins.

**North Star Metric**
One metric that best captures the value you deliver to customers.

**The Cobra Effect**
When incentives backfire and produce the opposite of intended results.
Marketing application: Test incentive structures carefully. More reviews doesn't mean better reviews if you incentivize quantity over quality.

## Growth & Scaling Models

**Feedback Loops**
Positive loops accelerate growth; negative loops create decline.
Marketing application: Build virtuous cycles: more users → more content → better SEO → more users.

**Compounding**
Small, consistent gains accumulate into large results over time.
Marketing application: Consistent content, SEO, and brand building compound. Start early; benefits accumulate exponentially.

**Network Effects**
A product becomes more valuable as more people use it.

**Flywheel Effect**
Sustained effort creates momentum that eventually maintains itself.
Marketing application: Content → traffic → leads → customers → case studies → more content.

**Switching Costs**
The price of changing to a competitor creates retention.
Marketing application: Increase switching costs ethically: integrations, data accumulation, workflow customization.

**Exploration vs. Exploitation**
Balance trying new things with optimizing what works.

**Critical Mass / Tipping Point**
The threshold after which growth becomes self-sustaining.
Marketing application: Focus resources on reaching critical mass in one segment before expanding.

**Survivorship Bias**
Focusing on successes while ignoring failures.
Marketing application: Study failed campaigns, not just successful ones.
